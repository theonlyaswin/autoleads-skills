# Proposal Structure

A good freelance proposal is about the client, not the freelancer. It shows you understood the problem, makes the scope impossible to misread, and makes saying yes easy.

## Sections

```markdown
# <Project name> — Proposal for <Client>

Prepared by <Sender> · <date> · Valid until <date + 14 days>

## Where you are
2–4 sentences: their situation, the problem, why now. Use their words from the post or call.

## What you want to achieve
3–5 bullets of outcomes (not tasks). "Customers can subscribe from the product page", "Launch before 20 Nov".

## How I'll approach it
3–5 short steps. One sentence each. Mention one relevant past project if the sender has one.

## Options

| | Essential | Recommended | Complete |
|---|---|---|---|
| <deliverable> | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| <deliverable> | – | ✓ | ✓ |
| <deliverable> | – | – | ✓ |
| Timeline | 2 weeks | 3 weeks | 5 weeks |
| Revision rounds | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| **Price** | **<cur> X** | **<cur> Y** | **<cur> Z** |

One sentence on why Recommended is recommended.

## Not included
Bullet list of things a reasonable client might assume are included but aren't (copywriting, stock photos, hosting fees, ongoing maintenance, third-party licences, content entry beyond N items, etc.). Each can be added later as a change request.

## Timeline
| Week | Milestone | You get |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kick-off & discovery | Sitemap + wireframes |
| … | … | … |

What the timeline depends on: feedback within N business days, access to X by <date>.

## Investment & terms
- Payment: 50% to start, 50% on delivery (or per milestone for projects over ~4 weeks).
- Invoices due within 7 days.
- Revisions: as listed per option; extra rounds or new requests are quoted separately before work starts.
- Ownership: full rights transfer on final payment.
- Valid until <date>.

## Next step
One sentence: "Reply with the option you'd like and I'll send the agreement and first invoice. We can start <date>."
```

## Pricing guidance

- Anchor high to low in the table only if the user prefers; default left-to-right is Essential → Complete.
- Spread: Essential ≈ 60–70% of Recommended; Complete ≈ 140–180%. Only use these ratios to shape options from the user's own rate — never to invent a base price.
- Essential must still be a real, useful result, not a crippled version.
- Complete adds things the client actually mentioned wanting later (phase 2 items from the call), not random upsells.
- Round prices (2,400 not 2,371).
- If the user charges hourly, show an estimated range of hours per option and a cap, not open-ended.
- Retainers: state hours or deliverables per month, rollover policy, and notice period.

## Single-quote variant

Drop the options table; keep one deliverables list, one price, and the Not included list. Use when the client asked for "a quote" for a fixed, well-defined job.

## Tone

- Plain, confident, specific. Short sentences.
- No "I am a highly skilled…", no "synergy", no "leverage", no exclamation marks.
- Match the client's language if they don't write in English.

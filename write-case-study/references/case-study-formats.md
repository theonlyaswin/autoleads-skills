# Case Study Formats

## Case study (300–500 words)

```markdown
# <Result-first title, e.g. "Launching subscriptions for a DTC coffee brand in 3 weeks">

**Client:** <name or anonymous description> · **Services:** <e.g. Shopify development, Recharge> · **Timeline:** <e.g. 3 weeks>

## The challenge
2–3 short paragraphs. Their situation, what was at stake, why the obvious fix didn't work. Make the reader who has the same problem nod.

## The approach
3–5 bullets or short paragraphs: the key decisions and why. One obstacle and how it was handled shows competence better than a list of tools.

## The result
Concrete outcomes. Numbers if real; otherwise checkable facts (shipped date, what's now possible, what's no longer needed).

> "<client quote, verbatim>" — <name, role> (only if real and approved)

**Want something similar?** <one-line CTA with the user's contact or booking link>
```

Writing rules:
- Lead with the client's problem, not the user's skills.
- Specific > impressive. "Cut checkout from 5 steps to 2" beats "dramatically improved UX".
- Plain language; explain any jargon a non-technical buyer wouldn't know.
- No invented metrics, ever. "Too early to measure" is honest and fine.

## LinkedIn post (120–200 words)

Shape:
1. Hook line that names the result or the tension. Under 12 words. ("A coffee brand asked me for subscriptions. They needed something else first.")
2. 3–6 short lines of story: problem → key decision → result.
3. One lesson other buyers or freelancers can use.
4. Soft CTA: "If you're planning something similar, happy to share how we set it up."

Line breaks between thoughts. Max 3 relevant hashtags at the end, or none. No "I'm thrilled to announce".

## X post

One tweet, under 280 characters: result + how + one detail. Thread variant: 1 hook, 2–3 story posts, 1 lesson, 1 CTA.

## Testimonial request

> Hi <name>, really enjoyed working on <project> with you. Would you be up for a short testimonial I can share with future clients? A couple of sentences is perfect. If it helps:
> - What was the situation before we worked together?
> - What was it like working together?
> - What's different now?
> Happy to draft something from our chats for you to edit if that's easier.

## Proof point

One sentence, under 25 words: what + for whom + concrete outcome. Used verbatim by DMs and proposals, so it must be true and publicly shareable.

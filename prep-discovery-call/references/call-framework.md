# Discovery Call Framework

The goal of a discovery call is not to pitch. It is to understand the problem well enough to write a proposal the client says yes to, and to find out early if the job is a bad fit.

The user should talk about 30% of the time. Questions do the selling.

## Agenda (30 minutes)

| Time | Block | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Open | Thank them, confirm the time, state the plan: "I'd like to understand what you need, share how I'd approach it, then agree next steps." |
| 3–18 | Their problem | Questions. Listen. Repeat back what you heard. |
| 18–25 | Your approach | One or two sentences on how you'd tackle it, one relevant example, rough shape of options. No final price on the call unless the user wants to. |
| 25–30 | Next steps | Confirm decision maker, timeline, and when the proposal lands. |

For a 15-minute intro: 2 / 9 / 2 / 2. For 45–60 minutes: expand the problem block, not the pitch.

## Question bank

Pick 8–12 that fit the lead; rewrite them in plain words for their situation.

**Problem**
- What made you post about this now? What changed?
- What have you tried so far? What didn't work?
- What happens if this doesn't get done?

**Success**
- ★ If this goes perfectly, what's different three months from now?
- How will you measure whether it worked?

**Scope**
- ★ What's a must-have for launch, and what can wait for a second phase?
- Is there existing work (code, designs, accounts, brand assets) I'd be building on?
- Who will I work with day to day, and how quickly can they give feedback?

**Timeline**
- ★ Is there a hard date driving this (launch, event, funding, season)?

**Budget**
- ★ Do you have a budget range in mind, or should I give you a few options at different levels?
- How have you paid for similar work before: fixed price, hourly, retainer?

**Decision**
- Who else needs to sign off before you start?
- Are you talking to other freelancers or agencies? What would make you choose one?

## Red flags

Mention only those relevant to the lead; give the user a polite way to probe each.

- **No budget, or "it's a quick job"** for clearly large work → anchor with options early.
- **Unclear decision maker** → ask who signs off; offer to include them in the proposal walk-through.
- **"We had a bad experience with the last freelancer"** → ask what went wrong; it shows what they'll expect.
- **Spec work / free trial requests** → offer a small paid first milestone instead.
- **Rush with no reason** → confirm the date is real before promising it.
- **Scope keeps growing on the call** → name phases: "Let's put that in phase 2."
- **Equity-only or "great exposure"** → only if the user explicitly accepts that.

## Close

End every call with a concrete, dated next step:
- "I'll send a proposal with two or three options by Thursday. Does that work?"
- "Can you send me access to X so I can include it in the estimate?"
- Confirm the best channel for the proposal (email, LinkedIn, etc.).

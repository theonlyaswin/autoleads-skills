# LinkedIn Profile Rubric

The standard every audit is judged against. Compiled from LinkedIn's own profile guidance, university career centres and recruiter / freelancer research (2024–2026). Treat the stats as directional: LinkedIn has published them over the years and the exact multipliers vary by source.

Who the profile is for: a **buyer** (client, hiring manager, founder) who clicked through from a DM, a comment or a search, and decides in about 10 seconds whether this person can solve their problem. Everything is judged by: *would the user's target client get it, believe it, and know what to do next?*

## Weights

| Section | Weight | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 20 | Shows next to every comment, DM and search result. The single most-read line. |
| Photo | 12 | Profiles with a photo get far more views and connection requests (LinkedIn has cited ~21× views, ~9× requests). |
| About | 15 | The pitch. Only the first ~2–3 lines show before "see more". |
| Experience | 12 | Proof they've done it. Recruiter and buyer searches weight titles and recent roles. |
| Featured | 10 | Portfolio above the fold. The fastest way to show, not tell. |
| Banner | 8 | Prime real estate most people leave as the default blue. |
| Skills | 8 | Search discoverability. Profiles with 5+ skills are found and messaged far more often. |
| Recommendations | 7 | Third-party proof. Buyers trust clients more than self-description. |
| Services / Open to | 4 | Lets buyers find and request them directly. |
| Activity & basics | 4 | Custom URL, location, contact info, recent activity. |

Overall = Σ (section score / 10 × weight). Sections "not seen" are left out and the total is rescaled to /100; say so.

## Scoring guide (every section)

- **9–10** Clear, specific to the target client, backed by proof, nothing to fix but polish.
- **7–8** Solid; one or two specific improvements.
- **4–6** Present but generic, unclear or unproven. Costs them clients.
- **1–3** Actively hurts: default, empty-feeling, off-target, unprofessional.
- **0** Missing.

---

## Headline (20)

**Good**
- Formula: **who you help + what you do / outcome + proof or niche**. e.g. "Shopify developer for DTC brands | Faster stores, higher conversion | 40+ stores launched".
- Key phrase (the words their buyers type into search) in the **first ~60 characters**: that's what shows in comments, DM lists and mobile.
- Uses words buyers search: the service ("Webflow designer", "B2B copywriter"), and "freelance" / "consultant" / "agency" if relevant.
- Specific over impressive. Up to 220 characters available; use it, but front-load.

**Bad**
- Default "<Title> at <Company>" (LinkedIn fills this in if left alone).
- Buzzwords with no content: "Passionate", "Visionary", "Guru", "Ninja", "Helping businesses grow".
- Lists of 6 unrelated roles ("Designer | Developer | Marketer | Photographer | Coach").
- "Seeking opportunities" / "Unemployed" when they're selling services.
- Emoji walls; one is fine if it matches their brand.

## Photo (12)

**Good**
- Recent, looks like them today. Face fills roughly 60% of the frame, head and shoulders.
- Eyes to camera, natural smile or friendly expression, good even light (daylight facing a window works).
- Plain or softly blurred background, high resolution, not pixelated.
- Dressed the way they'd show up to a client call in their field.

**Bad**
- No photo, logo or avatar instead of a face, group crop, selfie at arm's length, sunglasses, heavy filters, dark or backlit, far away, tilted, wedding / holiday photo, visibly old.

Fix advice: phone portrait mode + window light + plain wall + a friend is enough.

## Banner (8)

**Good**
- Custom image, 1584 × 396 px, sharp.
- One clear value line ("Shopify stores that sell. For DTC brands.") and optionally proof (logos only with permission, a stat) or a CTA ("Book a call: name.com").
- Text kept to the right/centre: the photo covers the bottom-left on desktop and more on mobile.
- Matches their brand colours / portfolio.

**Bad**
- Default blue/grey, random stock photo (city skyline, laptop on desk), unreadable small text, blurry stretched image, text hidden behind the photo.

## About (15)

**Good**
- **First 2 lines** (~200–300 characters, what shows before "see more") hook the target client: their problem or the result the user delivers. Not "I am a passionate…".
- First person, conversational, short paragraphs and white space.
- Structure: who you help & the problem → what you do and how → proof (results, clients, years, numbers, a short story) → who it's *not* for (optional, builds trust) → **clear CTA** (DM me, book a call, email, link).
- Keywords from the headline repeated naturally (search reads this section).
- Up to ~2,600 characters available; 1,000–2,000 is usually the sweet spot.

**Bad**
- Empty, one line, or a third-person bio. A copy of the CV / experience list. A wall of text. Every sentence about the user, none about the buyer. No CTA. Unverifiable superlatives.

## Featured (10)

**Good**
- 3–5 items, best first: case study, portfolio / live site link, a strong post that shows expertise, a testimonial graphic, a lead magnet or booking link.
- Each has a clear title and thumbnail that says what it is.
- Relevant to what they sell now.

**Bad**
- Missing (wasted space above the fold), outdated items, unrelated reposts, broken links, generic thumbnails.

## Experience (12)

**Good**
- Current role clearly states what they do now (for freelancers: "Freelance <service>" or their studio name, with a one-line description of who they help).
- 2–5 bullets per recent role written as **outcomes** with numbers where real ("Rebuilt checkout for a coffee brand, conversion up 18%"), not duties.
- Relevant media attached (links, images, documents).
- Titles use the words buyers search. No unexplained gaps that confuse the story; older roles can be short.

**Bad**
- Titles only, no descriptions. Duties ("Responsible for…"). Freelance work hidden or missing. Inconsistent with headline (headline says designer, experience says sales).

## Skills (8)

**Good**
- At least 5, ideally 15–30, all relevant to what they sell.
- Top skills pinned / listed first are the ones their buyers search (service + core tools).
- Related tools grouped (Shopify + Liquid + Recharge reads more credible than Shopify alone).
- Skills tied to the experience where they were used.

**Bad**
- Fewer than 5, irrelevant or outdated skills on top (MS Word, "Teamwork"), soft-skill padding.

## Recommendations (7)

**Good**
- 2+ recent recommendations from clients or managers, specific about results and what it was like to work together.
- Ideally from people like their target client.

**Bad**
- None, only from peers/friends, very old, vague ("Great guy to work with").

Fix advice: ask 2–3 happy past clients with a short message that makes it easy (offer prompts: before, working together, after).

## Services / Open to (4)

**Good (freelancers / agencies)**
- Services page set up (Add profile section → Services) with the actual services listed and a short description: it makes them findable in LinkedIn's service search and adds a "Request services" button.
- If job hunting instead: "Open to work" set to the right roles, location and type (contract / full-time), recruiters-only if they prefer discretion.

**Bad**
- Selling services with no Services page. "Open to work" banner on a profile that's trying to look in-demand to clients (it signals availability problems to some buyers; recruiters-only is safer).

## Activity & basics (4)

**Good**
- Custom URL (linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname, no random digits).
- Location set (search filters by it), including if they work remotely for a specific market.
- Contact info / website filled in.
- Some recent activity: posts or thoughtful comments in their niche in the last month or two.
- Name field is just their name (no emails, phone numbers or slogans in the name).

**Bad**
- Default URL with numbers, no location, no website, no activity for a year, slogans in the name field.

---

## Top-3 fixes: how to pick

Rank by **impact on the target client ÷ effort**. Usually, in order:
1. Headline (minutes, seen everywhere).
2. Photo or banner if either scores ≤ 4.
3. About's first 2 lines + CTA.
4. Featured with one real case study.
5. Recommendations (slow, so start the asks now).

## Red flags to always call out

- Headline and About contradict each other or the user's stated offer.
- Contact info, email or phone in the name field.
- Typos and grammar errors in the top card or first lines of About.
- Claims they can't back up ("#1 designer", "guaranteed 10× ROI").
- Anything that looks AI-generated and generic (stacked buzzwords, "In today's fast-paced digital landscape…").
